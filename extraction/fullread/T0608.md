## 2. Eligibility
Core, at the weakest end. A generative LLM (GPT-4) sits on the operator side of an economic model predictive control (MPC) loop and turns the MPC's planned day-ahead climate reference trajectories into plain-language summaries for growers (Figure 1, p.4; p.3). It advises only; it does not touch the controller. One setup is tested: 50 GPT-4 summaries scored with BERTScore against the template-generated long reports (p.8, p.12). Domain is a greenhouse (adjacent, controlled-environment agriculture). The plant and the MPC are simulated (p.3).

## 3. What was built, in plain words
The plant is a simulated Venlo-type semi-closed tomato greenhouse whose model is fitted to a Humboldt University Berlin facility (p.3). An economic MPC, from the authors' earlier work, maximises yield minus production cost under climate bounds, using 2011 Berlin weather-station data and energy prices. It produces time-varying reference trajectories for air temperature, CO2 and relative humidity, which go to the greenhouse's local climate controller, and that controller moves the heating, ventilation, CO2 injection and cooling (p.3, Figure 1). The data are 5-minute points over each day, March to October 2011 (p.3).

The language support system sits in the digital interface next to the local controller and only reads the trajectories (Figure 1, p.4). It runs in three stages (Figure 2). (1) Change-point detection (CPD) splits each trajectory into segments. The supervised options are PELT, CUMSUM and EWMA, from the ruptures package. The unsupervised option is K-means clustering of extreme points, with EVT-style Gaussian smoothing and a weighted hybrid (pp.4–6). (2) Slopes between change points are labelled with fixed categories (for example "sharp increase", 0.1 to 0.5) (Table 1, p.6). (3) A deterministic template fills slots such as "From {start} to {end}, the CO2 experienced {description} from {x} to {y} ppm" to build a long narrative report (Figure 4, p.9; Box 1, p.15). The authors chose templates over an LLM at this stage for determinism (p.7).

GPT-4, called through the OpenAI API at temperature 0.2 with no fine-tuning, then condenses the long report into a short daily summary using a predefined prompt (p.7). The prompt text is not given. Nothing checks GPT-4's output before the grower reads it, and there is no actuation path from the LLM.

## 4. Each tested setup
- **Setup 1: GPT-4 summarises the template reports of MPC reference trajectories (advisory).** Input reports come from both the PELT and the K-means hybrid segmentations (Boxes 2–3, p.16). Evaluation: 50 randomly sampled pairs of long report and summary, scored with BERTScore, a semantic-similarity metric (p.8, p.12). There is no comparator: no non-LLM summariser, no other LLM and no human rating. There are no control metrics and no off-nominal tests. Each summary is generated once; the paper reports no run-to-run variance. Nothing is varied for the LLM.
- Not LLM setups, reported for context: the CPD methods are compared on F1 against manual labels from 20 random days (Table 2, p.8; Table 4, p.14) and on piecewise-linear R² (Table 3, p.11).

## 5. Results
- LLM summary fidelity: "an average BERTScore of 0.82 across 50 randomly selected samples" (p.12). This measures semantic similarity between each summary and its source report, on a 0 to 1 scale where higher is better. The denominator is 50 summaries. No spread is given, and the score was rounded (p.13).
- The example outputs contain visible errors that the authors do not discuss. Box 3 (p.16) says humidity was "mostly maintaining around 89% to 89%" and biomass "a steady increase from 14 kg/m² to 14 kg/m²". The two summaries for June 13 disagree on the opening temperature (16°C in Box 2, 14°C in Box 3), although both should come from the same MPC trajectory. These are anecdotes, not a measured error rate.
- CPD (non-LLM): PELT F1,avg 0.2464, CUMSUM 0.0889, EWMA 0.0351 (Table 2, p.8). The K-means hybrid reached F1 of 0.587 (T), 0.546 (C) and 0.526 (H), against PELT's 0.0909, 0.3636 and 0.2857 (Table 4, p.14). Higher is better.
- The authors' own limitation: "BERTScore provides a computational semantic similarity measure, it may not fully capture the nuanced requirements of greenhouse operations" (p.15). Human evaluation is left to future work (p.16).
- The paper does not show the LLM beating any non-LLM alternative on anything. No grower usability or decision outcome is measured.

## 6. Duplicates
The MPC framework and greenhouse model come from Sathyanarayanan et al. (2024) (p.3), a non-LLM control paper. No other LLM paper reporting this experiment is known from the text.

## 7. Terms to define
- Reference trajectory: a time-varying setpoint profile (T, CO2, RH) planned by the MPC and sent to the local climate controller.
- Change-point detection (CPD): finding times where a time series' statistical behaviour changes; here used to split a trajectory into trend segments.
- PELT: Pruned Exact Linear Time, a penalised optimal-segmentation CPD algorithm (penalty 10, jump 6 here).
- EVT: Extreme Value Theory; here a pipeline of Gaussian smoothing, local-extremum detection and clustering of extremes.
- NLG (template-based): natural language generation that fills fixed sentence templates with numbers.
- BERTScore: a text-similarity score based on BERT embeddings; it measures semantic overlap, not factual correctness or usefulness.
- Venlo greenhouse: a common glass greenhouse design; semi-closed means ventilation is limited and climate is actively conditioned.

### 4.1 Operator interface and explanation
- **Short version:** Naagarajan et al. put GPT-4 on the operator side of a simulated economic MPC for a tomato greenhouse. Template reports of the MPC's day-ahead temperature, CO2 and humidity setpoints were condensed into grower summaries, with a mean BERTScore of 0.82 over 50 summaries against their source reports. <!-- p.7 --> <!-- p.12 -->
- **Long version:** Naagarajan et al. used GPT-4 only to explain, never to act. An economic MPC planned 5-minute setpoint trajectories for a simulated tomato greenhouse. Change-point detection and fixed sentence templates first turned these into long reports, and GPT-4 (temperature 0.2) then condensed them. <!-- p.3 --> <!-- p.7 --> The only test was semantic similarity: a mean BERTScore of 0.82 over 50 summaries, with no human rating and no baseline. <!-- p.12 --> The printed examples show why that is not enough. Two summaries of the same day give different starting temperatures. <!-- p.16 --> Nothing checked the summaries before a grower read them. This is the gap a review should name: explanation layers are tested for fluency, not for factual fidelity to the controller's plan.
