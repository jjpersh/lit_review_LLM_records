## 2. Eligibility
Core. A generative LLM (Qwen3-8B, fine-tuned) acts as a supervisory coordinator: every interval it picks a discrete "mode" per controlled unit, and each mode is mapped to parameters of a conventional low-level controller (hysteresis-band width of ramp-metering gates; coefficients of an MPC-style MILP). The low-level layer then moves the actuators (p.3, pp.10–12). Tested in closed loop in simulation on two adjacent-domain plants: freeway ramp metering (traffic) and a virtual power plant (VPP) on a distribution feeder (p.5). Not a process system; both are adjacent (traffic; grid/building energy).

## 3. What was built, in plain words
Two simulated plants. (1) An 8-ramp urban expressway corridor (Western Expressway, Changchun). Each ramp has an on/off gate driven by a hysteresis rule on merge-area occupancy: close above an upper threshold, open below a lower one (pp.10–11). (2) A VPP: PV, an 1800 kWh battery, an EV fleet and HVAC in an industrial park, dispatched every 5 min by a rolling-horizon mixed-integer linear program (MILP, 60-min window, solved with CBC) (pp.11–12). The LLM never writes an actuator command. Every 3 min (traffic) or 30 min (VPP) it reads a structured natural-language prompt (current measurements, recent trends, forecast with stated reliability, uncertainty level, priorities, events, mode rules) and emits one mode per unit: Sensitive/Standard/Sluggish/Always-Open per ramp (sets band width 0.04/0.10/0.20 or pins the gate open), or Conservative/Balanced/Proactive per VPP resource (sets PV forecast de-rating, battery reserve and discharge cap, EV and comfort penalty weights) (pp.11–12, 15, 18). A schema validator checks the output; an invalid output keeps the previous decision for one interval (p.13). The MILP holds physical bounds as hard constraints; in the feeder simulator, commands that would breach voltage or line limits are curtailed by bisection (pp.12, 21). Gate actuation is on/off; VPP powers are continuous. The policy is Qwen3-8B with LoRA, fine-tuned by "Continuation-Aware GRPO": each candidate decision is applied for one interval and then scored over a 4-interval rollout under a frozen copy of the policy (pp.3–5, 17). Training uses fast simplified simulators (link transmission model; single-bus model); testing uses SUMO and OpenDSS (p.5).

## 4. Each tested setup
**Setup A: multi-ramp traffic control, SUMO.** 300 test scenarios (100 seen-pattern, 100 unseen-pattern with shifted/double peaks, 100 higher-uncertainty with demand noise 5%→15% and forecast error 8%→20%), five matched runs, each 3 h plus 15-min warm-up (pp.5–6, 22–23). Comparators: published feedback controller (all ramps at Standard, from a field study), end-to-end PPO, Fixed-Mode Hierarchy (best static mode vector from 64 group-wise candidates), Hierarchical RL (PPO over the same mode space, grid-searched hyperparameters), prompting-only Qwen3-8B, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5 (pp.5, 18–20). Metric: vehicles discharged (higher better); auxiliary bottleneck speed, congestion minutes, ramp queue, spillovers, longest closure (pp.25–26). Varied: test subset, simulator (LTM vs SUMO), continuation horizon H (∆ to 8∆), context blocks withheld.

**Setup B: VPP energy management, OpenDSS EPRI Ckt5 feeder.** Same split and run structure, 10:00–22:00 episodes in cooling season (pp.5, 11). Comparators: MPC-MILP with a fixed neutral coefficient set (outside the mode space), end-to-end PPO, Fixed-Mode Hierarchy (exhaustive over 81 vectors), Hierarchical RL, the three prompting-only LLMs (pp.19–20). Metric: realised operating cost in CNY (lower better), including over-cap import, comfort and EV-shortfall penalties; auxiliary voltage/thermal violation steps before curtailment, curtailed energy, unmet EV energy, comfort excursion (pp.24–26).

## 5. Results
- Traffic headline: "overall traffic throughput of 16625.4 ± 84.5, improving over Feedback Control by 6.62% and Hierarchical RL by 3.06%" (p.6). Mean over 5 runs, each the average of 300 scenarios.
- VPP headline: "operating cost of 32679.2 ± 223.6, reducing cost by 4.37% relative to MPC-MILP and by 2.49% relative to Hierarchical RL" (p.6).
- The LLM beats non-LLM alternatives: yes, on both tasks, against a tuned feedback controller, MPC-MILP, PPO end-to-end and Hierarchical RL. Paired bootstrap intervals all exclude zero; narrowest vs Hierarchical RL "[+408, +574] vehicles and [+688, +1002] CNY" (p.28). Ahead on all five seeds except horizon ablation on throughput (4/5) (p.28).
- Prompting-only LLMs do not beat the conventional baseline: traffic gains −2.06% (Qwen3-8B), +0.43% (Gemini), +0.13% (Claude) vs feedback control (Table 1, p.6); VPP all worse than MPC-MILP, −7.91%, −2.85%, −3.36% (Table 2, p.6).
- Robustness: seen-to-high-uncertainty degradation "decreases by 4.86%, compared with 8.14%–11.87% for the baselines" (traffic); VPP "4.94%, compared with 8.52%–16.74%" (p.6).
- Cross-simulator gap (fast to realistic, lower better): "smallest gaps of 3.82 ± 0.33% and 5.62 ± 0.54%" (p.7). Non-learned feedback control and MPC-MILP have the next-smallest gaps, 4.31% and 6.94% (p.25, Table 22). Corridor transfer is not zero-shot: hysteresis centres and capacity were calibrated on SUMO training data (pp.20–21).
- Ablation: continuation horizon H=4∆ vs H=∆ gives "1.88% gain" throughput and "a reduction of 2.69%" in cost (p.7). Compute-matched short-horizon control recovers only +0.52% and +0.84% (p.26).
- Decision validity (count over all test decisions): schema-violation rate 0.03% corridor / 0.00% VPP for the fine-tuned policy vs 4.7% / 3.1% for untuned Qwen3-8B (Table 21, p.26); "fewer than one malformed output in 3,000 decisions" (p.25).
- Constraint side, VPP: pre-curtailment violation steps 5.2 ± 0.8 (ours) vs 9.4 (MPC-MILP), 18.3 (untuned Qwen3-8B) per 144-step episode; curtailed energy 241 vs 412 kWh (Table 20, p.26).
- Context ablation: measurements-only prompt still beats direct control by "2.51% on throughput and 0.45% on cost" (p.28).
- Failure case: comfort-limited VPP family gain 0.3%, bootstrap "[−148, +361] CNY", no claimed improvement; "MPC-MILP is the appropriate choice" (p.30).
- Timing: one decision "about 1.6 s for the corridor and 0.4 s for the VPP", against intervals of 3 min and 30 min (p.20).

## 7. Terms to define
- Hysteresis gate: on/off ramp gate that closes above an upper occupancy threshold and opens below a lower one; band width δ sets how readily it switches.
- Virtual power plant (VPP): aggregated PV, battery, EV charging and HVAC run as one dispatchable unit.
- MILP / MPC-MILP: mixed-integer linear program solved on a rolling horizon, i.e. MPC with integer variables.
- GRPO: Group Relative Policy Optimization; RL fine-tuning that scores a group of sampled LLM outputs for the same prompt against each other, without a value model.
- Continuation-Aware GRPO: GRPO where each decision is scored over its own interval plus several later intervals under a frozen copy of the policy.
- LoRA: low-rank adapter fine-tuning of an LLM.
- LTM: link transmission model, a fast macroscopic traffic model; SUMO: microscopic vehicle-level traffic simulator.
- OpenDSS / EPRI Ckt5: open-source distribution-feeder power-flow simulator and a standard test feeder.
- PPO: Proximal Policy Optimization, the RL algorithm used for non-LLM baselines.
- Joint mode: the vector of discrete modes the LLM emits, one per controlled unit.
