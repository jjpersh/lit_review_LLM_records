## 2. Eligibility
Eligible as core. A generative LLM (Claude Sonnet, named on p.23) sits at Layer 3 of a supervisory fault-tolerant control architecture. When a fault is detected, it picks a controller mode from a bounded set. In the mechanical benchmark it overrode the rule-based mode choice and the plant ran on its pick (p.16, p.24). Both plants are simulated: a 4th-order coupled oscillator that stands in for compressor and pipeline dynamics, and a 1-D thermal steel-sheet heating process. Both count as process systems, not an adjacent domain.

## 3. What was built, in plain words
Everything is simulated. The actuator is driven by a conventional continuous controller: an LQI (linear quadratic integral state feedback, designed by solving the Riccati equation) on the mechanical plant, or a PI heat-flux controller on the thermal plant. Above it, a "Layer 2" supervisor runs every time step (dt = 0.01 s). It compares the measured state with a one-step prediction from the nominal model. The authors call this prediction a "lightweight digital twin" (p.6–7). The supervisor combines tracking error, the model residual and the previous control effort into an anomaly score, and computes a logistic "risk" value. An event fires when the score exceeds κ times its nominal settled value AND either the score rate or the risk is high. The supervisor then picks from a bank of pre-designed, Hurwitz-verified controllers: nominal, degraded, or "energy-save" (degraded gains plus an effort cap). A dwell-time counter blocks immediate switching back (p.8).

The LLM ("Layer 3") is called once per fault event, 0.4 s after detection. It receives a structured summary of the detection state and must return JSON with one of four actions (keep_nominal, switch_degraded, energy_save_mode, request_human_review), a confidence level and a short rationale. The prompt is given in Appendix A, temperature is zero, and there is no fine-tuning or tool use (p.8, p.24, p.28). If the LLM returns an action outside the allowed set, the Layer 2 decision is kept. If the call fails or times out, the system falls back to the nominal controller (p.9). Algorithm A1 sets mode ← LLM_call (p.29), so the LLM's choice is applied without human approval. The paper contradicts itself here: p.27 says switching is "determined entirely by deterministic supervisory logic". Actuation is continuous. The mode switch is discrete.

## 4. Each tested setup
**Setup 1: Mechanical benchmark with LLM layer (nominal plant plus variants A–C).** Each run lasts 25 s with a unit step reference at 2 s. There are four scenarios (p.11): nominal; gradual damping drift (θ from 1 to 0.15 over 8–18 s); abrupt damping loss at 10 s; and abrupt 75% actuator loss (α = 0.25) at 10 s. The comparators are the LQI alone (tuned by Riccati cost matrices), SIGS (score-interpolated gain scheduling), CFG (0.5× LQI gains), an MPC (N = 30, same costs, u ∈ [−8, 8]), MPC+Cognitive, an RLS adaptive fault-tolerant controller, and Cognitive without the LLM (p.11–12, p.16). Metrics are IAE, ISE, control energy, overshoot, settling time and detection time (p.14–15). Plant variants A (unstable), B (stiff) and C (near-undamped) are tested under the actuator fault (p.12–13). The sensitivity sweeps cover α, κ, onset time, noise (Monte Carlo, 50 seeds × 6 levels), score weights and dwell time (p.19). The paper does not say whether the LLM was active in the sweeps. Each LLM-enabled scenario is a single deterministic run with one LLM call.

**Setup 2: Thermal steel-sheet process with LLM layer.** A 1-D heat equation with 41 nodes is run for 15 s. The PI loop runs at 1 ms and tracks 200 °C at mid-thickness (p.13). There are four scenarios: nominal; heater power loss to 25% at 5 s; gradual fouling (h rising to 5× over 3–8 s); and a 40% drop in thermal diffusivity at 5 s (p.13–14). The comparators are the nominal PI (labelled "LQI"), SIGS and CFG. The only metric is IAE (p.22). A fault-type heuristic routes the fault to a degraded or a phase-change PI.

**Setup 3: AFTC+LLM.** An RLS-based adaptive fault-tolerant controller runs with an LLM audit call under the abrupt actuator fault (p.16–17). The LLM only comments on the adaptive response. One run.

## 5. Results
- Actuator fault, mechanical (p.15–16): "The cognitive controller achieves overshoot of 0.094 and IAE of 2.313—the best among all methods." IAE is lower-is-better. The comparators score LQI 2.366, SIGS 2.332, CFG 3.214, MPC 7.089 and RLS-AFTC 2.364 (p.16). This is the framework, mostly Layer 2. The gain of the full framework over LQI is 2.3%.
- LLM contribution (p.24): "The effect on IAE is marginal (2.312 versus 2.313)". The LLM picked energy_save_mode over the surrogate's switch_degraded. Cognitive with and without the LLM is listed as identical (2.313) in Table 1 (p.9).
- Non-intrusiveness (p.15): the framework is "numerically identical to a standalone LQI, with zero supervisory interventions" in scenarios 1–3. MPC+Cognitive gives a false trigger at 8.01 s in all three.
- Plant C (p.19): "IAE = 2.363 versus LQI's 2.980, a 20.7% reduction". The LLM chose switch_degraded with high confidence (p.24). Plant A stayed dormant (0%). Plant B had a false trigger at 8.01 s, before the fault (p.18–19).
- Noise Monte Carlo (p.19): "Zero false positives were observed across all 300 nominal-scenario runs". ΔIAE was significant up to σ = 0.05.
- Thermal (p.22): power loss "IAE = 16.89 versus the LQI's 20.70, an 18.4% improvement". Phase change was worse (18.72 vs 16.72, −11.9%). Fouling showed 0% change. The LLM confirmed switch_degraded with medium confidence (p.23).
- LLM decision record (p.24): "Across the three supervisory events reported in Table 5, the LLM selected actions consistent with the surrogate". The denominator is 3 calls. request_human_review was never triggered (p.25).
- AFTC+LLM (p.17): the LLM "produces no IAE change (IAE = 2.364)".
- LLM versus non-LLM: the full framework beats tuned LQI, MPC, CFG, SIGS and RLS-AFTC on IAE and overshoot under actuator loss. This advantage comes from the non-LLM supervisor. The paper itself says the LLM adds no measurable control performance (0.001 IAE).

## 7. Terms to define
- LQI: linear quadratic integral, i.e. LQR state feedback augmented with an integrator on the tracking error.
- CARE: continuous algebraic Riccati equation, used to compute the LQI gains.
- Digital twin (as used here): the one-step prediction of the nominal linear model, whose residual against the measured state is the fault signal.
- Anomaly score / risk: a weighted sum of |e|, the residual and u²; risk is a logistic function of these features plus the score rate.
- SIGS: score-interpolated gain scheduling, a baseline that blends nominal and degraded gains in proportion to the anomaly score.
- CFG: conservative fixed-gain baseline at 0.5× the nominal gains.
- Dwell time: the minimum time spent in a mode before the next switch. The average dwell-time bound τ*D = 11.03 s.
- Energy-save mode: degraded gains plus a cap on control effort.
- AFTC: adaptive fault-tolerant control using a recursive-least-squares estimate of actuator effectiveness.
- Audit record: the LLM's natural-language rationale, logged at each supervisory event.
