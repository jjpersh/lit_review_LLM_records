## 2. Eligibility
Core. A fine-tuned small language model (SLM, Qwen2.5-1.5B) chooses the heater power level of a simulated single-zone thermal system at every step, inside a closed loop with a rule-based validator and a second SLM that writes corrective hints (p.2–3). The paper tests this in 30 randomized closed-loop simulations of 500 steps, a 1,500-sample cross-model benchmark and a symbolic re-mapping stress test (p.4–5). The plant is a first-order room/thermal energy balance (p.3), so it is an adjacent domain (building/thermal energy) rather than a process unit, though the authors frame it as industrial control.

## 3. What was built, in plain words
The plant is a simulated first-order thermal system, C dT/dt = UA(Tout − T) + a + ε, with C = 20.0, UA = 0.5 (p.3–4). The manipulated variable is a discrete heating/cooling power a ∈ {−2Q, −Q, 0, Q, 2Q}, Q = 5.0, so actuation is multi-level on/off, not continuous (p.3). There is no PID or MPC.

At each step the "Action Agent" (Qwen2.5-1.5B, warm-started by distillation from 5,000 DeepSeek-R1-671B reasoning traces, then trained with GRPO, Group Relative Policy Optimization, a reinforcement-learning method that scores groups of 8 sampled answers against a reward, p.3) reads the state (current temperature, outdoor temperature, target band, previous action) and outputs a chain-of-thought plus one action (p.2). The "Validation Agent" is not a model: it computes the action that a fixed expert rule table would take (Table I: safety override, boundary trigger, hysteresis latching, default 0) and flags the SLM output UNSAFE if it differs (p.2–3). If flagged, a "Reprompt Agent" (a second Qwen2.5-1.5B, fine-tuned on 5,000 DeepSeek-R1 corrections of the Action Agent's errors) writes a hint without giving the answer, and the Action Agent tries again. After Nmax failed tries (value NR) Algorithm 1 returns the rule's action (p.2, verified on the page image). So under Algorithm 1 only the rule-identical action ever reaches the plant. The heuristic rule-based controller (HRBC) is both the validator's ground truth and the stated performance ceiling (p.4).

## 4. Each tested setup
**Setup A: closed-loop randomized simulation, SLM + rule validator + reprompting.** 30 randomized experiments of 500 steps (15,000 steps), unseen initial temperature 0–45 °C, setpoint 0–40 °C and ambient disturbance Tout from −20 to 40 °C (p.4). Ablation: untrained Qwen2.5-1.5B in the same loop over 2,000 samples (Table III, p.5). Case study with T0 = 42 °C, setpoint 25 °C, compared against HRBC, GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 trajectories (Figs 5–6, p.5); cold and hot start sweeps (Tout = 22 °C and 28 °C, Figs 7–8, p.5–6). Symbolic re-mapping stress test: the power label Q renamed F (p.5). Metrics: action-alignment accuracy against the rule table (higher better), mean inference latency, and in-range rate (IRR, share of steps inside the target band, higher better) for the re-mapping test only. Comparator: HRBC (the rule table itself), shown only as a trajectory overlay.

**Setup B: cross-model benchmark on 1,500 samples.** The same multi-agent loop with GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3 and the fine-tuned SLM as Action Agent (Table IV, p.4–5). Metrics: accuracy, mean latency, average reprompts per step. No non-LLM comparator; repeats NR. Whether this is closed-loop or a fixed state dataset is not stated beyond "a separate 1,500-sample benchmark" (p.4).

## 5. Results
- Training effect (Setup A): base model "achieved only 39.5% accuracy" versus "91.5%" after training (p.4; Table III, p.5). Denominators differ: 2,000 samples for the base model, 15,000 for the trained one. Both "include reprompting process" (Table III title), so these appear to be post-reprompt rates, though the paper does not say whether the final fallback is excluded.
- Spread across runs (Setup A): "91.5% average action-alignment accuracy (86.3%–100% across 30 experiments of 500 steps each)" (p.6).
- Regulation (Setup A, qualitative from figures): "stabilizes within the target safety zone (25°C ± 2°C) within 100 operational steps" (p.5); no IAE, overshoot or settling metric is tabulated.
- Re-mapping (Setup A): "exact action alignment accuracy decreased to 74.1%, the system achieved an In-Range Rate (IRR) of 95%" (p.5). Number of steps or runs NR. Since Algorithm 1 only executes rule-identical actions, 74.1% accuracy alongside executed non-rule actions implies the validator was not enforcing in this test; the paper does not explain.
- Cross-model (Setup B): GPT-4o 93.07%, 4.50 s, 0.16 reprompts; DeepSeek-V3 98.47%, 6.28 s, 0.09; SLM 96.67%, 3.84 s, 0.38 (Table IV, p.5). "reducing inference latency by 38.9% compared to DeepSeek-V3 and 14.7% compared to GPT-4o" (p.4–5). Lower latency is better; the SLM needs about four times as many reprompts as DeepSeek-V3.
- Latency is reported inconsistently: 3.84 s per step (Table IV, Fig. 6), "4.84s per step" (Fig. 5 axis), "average latency of 5.28 s" (Figs 7–8 captions, p.6). Hardware for SLM inference NR.
- Fig. 6 shows GPT-4o and DeepSeek trajectories deviating from HRBC (p.5), which again is hard to reconcile with a fallback-to-rule gate.
- The paper does not show the LLM beating a non-LLM alternative. HRBC is the reference and ceiling; at best the SLM reproduces it, more slowly (seconds per step versus a table lookup).

## 7. Terms to define
- SLM (small language model): here a 1.5-billion-parameter open-weight model run locally.
- GRPO: reinforcement-learning fine-tuning that samples a group of answers per state and rewards each relative to the group mean, without a value network.
- Action alignment accuracy: share of steps where the SLM's chosen power level equals the expert rule's choice.
- HRBC (heuristic rule-based controller): the Table I rule table run directly as the controller.
- Mission latching: hysteresis rule that keeps heating or cooling on until the setpoint band is crossed, to avoid chattering.
- IRR (in-range rate): percentage of steps with temperature inside the target band.
- Symbolic re-mapping: renaming the action label (Q to F) to test whether the model follows logic rather than tokens.
- Reprompt Agent / corrective hint: second SLM that tells the Action Agent where its reasoning failed without giving the answer.

**5.1 Head-to-head evidence**
- Short version: The only non-LLM comparator in Wang et al. is the rule table the model was trained to imitate; it serves as ground truth and ceiling, and the paper reports no case where the model beats it. <!-- p.4 -->
- Long version: not needed.

**5.2 Safeguards**
- Short version: Wang et al. gate every model action against an expert rule table and fall back to the rule's action after repeated mismatches, so by design only rule-identical actions reach the simulated plant. <!-- p.2 -->
- Long version: Wang et al. show why a validator's strength depends on what it checks against. Their gate compares each action from a fine-tuned 1.5-billion-parameter model with the action of a fixed rule table. On a mismatch, a second small model writes a hint and the first model tries again. After a set number of tries (not reported), the rule's action is applied. <!-- p.2 --> The gate was challenged by real bad proposals: the untrained model matched the rules on only 39.5% of steps. <!-- p.5 --> But a validator that already knows the right answer could simply run the plant, and the paper does not report how often the fallback fired. <!-- p.2 -->

**5.3 Timing**
- Short version: Wang et al. report a mean of 3.84 s per decision for their 1.5-billion-parameter model, against 4.50 s for GPT-4o and 6.28 s for DeepSeek-V3, but figure captions in the same paper give 4.84 s and 5.28 s per step, and the plant's time step is not stated. <!-- p.5 --> <!-- p.6 -->
- Long version: not needed.

**5.4 Local and open-weight models**
- Short version: Wang et al. fine-tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B for a simulated thermal loop. On 1,500 samples it matched expert rules on 96.67% of decisions, between GPT-4o (93.07%) and DeepSeek-V3 (98.47%), and responded fastest. <!-- p.5 -->
- Long version: Wang et al. test whether a small open-weight model can replace a cloud LLM in a control loop. They distilled Qwen2.5-1.5B from DeepSeek-R1 reasoning traces, then trained it with reinforcement learning against an expert rule table. <!-- p.3 --> Training raised rule agreement from 39.5% to 91.5% over 30 randomized closed-loop runs. <!-- p.5 --> On 1,500 samples, it reached 96.67% agreement, against 93.07% for GPT-4o and 98.47% for DeepSeek-V3. Its mean latency was 3.84 s, against 4.50 s and 6.28 s, though it needed 0.38 reprompts per step against 0.09 for DeepSeek-V3. <!-- p.5 --> All of this is in simulation, and the inference hardware is not reported. The authors list hardware-in-the-loop testing as future work. <!-- p.6 -->
