## 2. Eligibility
Yes. Generative LLMs (DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.7-Plus) and a fine-tuned small language model (Qwen3-0.6B) generate PID gains for a feedback loop. Each gain set is checked in closed-loop simulation against acceptance criteria and retained or rejected. Plants are simulated FOPDT and SOPDT models standing in for delayed chemical-process loops, so this is a process system, not adjacent. Recommend core.

## 3. What was built, in plain words
The plant is a simulated single loop. It is a first-order plus dead time (FOPDT) or second-order plus dead time (SOPDT) transfer function, integrated with Euler steps at Δt = 0.1 s over a 4000 s unit-setpoint step (p.16, p.40). No hardware is used. A parallel-form PID moves the (continuous) manipulated variable. The LLM never writes the actuator. It works as an offline or supervisory tuner that proposes the Kp, Ki and Kd triplet.

Each benchmark case starts from a deliberately mistuned PID: a perturbation of the balanced IMC controller from one of ten defect patterns (p.15). The loop is simulated, and a feature extractor computes IAE plus seven response features: overshoot, crossings, attenuation ratio, period, t63.2, settling time and steady-state error. Deterministic rules turn these features into engineering diagnoses ("inadequate damping", etc.). The prompt contains:
- the diagnoses, the numbers, current gains, dead time and requested style (conservative, balanced or aggressive);
- ten IMC-derived demonstration cases (p.10).

The LLM must return "P:<v>; I:<v>; D:<v>". The triplet is parsed and simulated, then accepted if it settles in ±5%, has overshoot <15% and has steady-state error <1% (p.8). If it fails, the remaining deficiencies go back as a feedback prompt, for up to 8 rounds.

The second arm fine-tunes Qwen3-0.6B. Step one is SFT on 40,000 records with simulation-verified IMC targets. Step two is PI-GRPO, a group-relative RL method whose reward is gated (pp.11–14):
- R = −1 for unparseable output, wrong gain signs, divergence, Routh–Hurwitz (Padé) instability, or a non-positive exact-delay phase margin;
- safe-but-failing candidates are scored in [−0.5, 0] and successful ones in [0.5, 1].

At inference, the same acceptance check applies. Hosted models are called through their APIs at temperature 0.1 with thinking disabled (p.50).

## 4. Each tested setup
**Setup A: hosted or unadapted LLM in the iterative observe–diagnose–adjust–validate loop (E1).**
- Test set: 100 FOPDT and 100 SOPDT cases (Latin hypercube sampling; dead time 1–200 s; four relative-delay strata; 10 defect types × severities of 60 mild, 100 moderate, 40 severe), all excluded from the demonstrations and training sets (pp.15–16).
- Runs: each case run once per model, up to 8 rounds. There are no repeated runs, and no seed was available for the hosted APIs (p.50).
- Models: DeepSeek-V4-Flash, Qwen3.7-Plus and Base Qwen3-0.6B.
- Comparator: balanced IMC tuning, a model-based tuned non-LLM method. It is shown only for 4 representative cases (C1–C4, Table 1). It is not reported batch-wide, but by construction the initial controller's IAE is ≥1.5× IMC's IAE.
- Metrics: Pass@k (share of cases where at least one of the first k recommendations meets all criteria), IAE, overshoot, steady-state error, and first-success iteration.
- What was varied:
  - prompt content (Full vs KPI-3 vs Numeric-8 ablation, p.24);
  - demonstration style (conservative, balanced or aggressive) for DeepSeek on cases C1 and C3 (pp.25–27).

**Setup B: Qwen3-0.6B adapted by SFT and PI-GRPO, a local model trained with a physics-informed stability-gated reward (E1).**
- Test set: the same 200 cases.
- Comparisons: Base, SFT and GRPO variants, against the hosted LLMs (Pass@1) and against IMC on the 4 representative cases (Table S2).
- Inference settings: vLLM, seed 42, temperature 0.1 (p.50).
- Also reported: exact-delay Bode and Nyquist phase margins for C1 and C3 (pp.30–32), and training curves including validation Pass@1 over GRPO steps (pp.27–28).

## 5. Results
- Setup A, Pass@8: "DeepSeek-V4-Flash achieves a Pass@8 of 75%, whereas Qwen3.7-Plus reaches 89%" (p.22). Denominator is 100 FOPDT cases. Higher is better.
- Setup A, SOPDT: "DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Qwen3.7-Plus achieve Pass@8 values of 79% and 77%" (p.22). Denominator is 100 SOPDT cases.
- Setup A, unadapted small model: "Base-0.6B achieves a Pass@8 of only 13%" on FOPDT and "succeeds in 18%" on SOPDT (pp.22–23). All its failures repeat the same PID triplet in every round. In addition, "9 of the 13 successful FOPDT controllers and 16 of the 18 successful SOPDT controllers exactly match" a demonstration triplet (p.23). This is copying, not tuning.
- Setup A, against IMC (representative cases): C1 "IMC and DeepSeek-V4-Flash reduce the IAE to 214.58 and 237.16" (p.19). In all four cases IMC has the lowest IAE: C2 92.65, C3 176.05, C4 16.20 (Table 1, pp.21–22). Lower IAE is better. **The LLMs do not beat the non-LLM IMC baseline on IAE in any reported case.** The only reported advantage is that the LLM works from response features without an identified process model, and the paper does not test that against IMC batch-wide.
- Setup A, prompt ablation (Table 2, p.25, Pass@8 hosted and Pass@1 Base):
  - FOPDT DeepSeek: 75 / 89 / 86 (Full / KPI-3 / Numeric-8);
  - SOPDT DeepSeek: "decreases from 79% with Full to 62% with Numeric-8" (p.24);
  - Base-0.6B SOPDT: 18 / 5 / 7.
  - Semantic diagnoses are not uniformly beneficial.
- Setup A, style steering: aggressive demonstrations gave overshoot "rises to 45.78%" for C1 and 16.53% for C3. Both controllers were "rejected under the common 15% overshoot criterion" (p.26).
- Setup B: "Base0.6B achieves a Pass@1 of only 15.5%. SFT increases this value to 86.5%, and PI-GRPO further improves it to 94.0%" (p.29). Denominator is 200 cases. Further detail:
  - PI-GRPO gained 20 cases and lost 5 relative to SFT;
  - both reach 95% final success;
  - hosted Pass@1 was "56.5% and 71.0%" (p.29).
- Setup B, trade-off: "Mean first-round IAE increases from 193.1 to 231.2 for FOPDT" (p.53), comparing SFT with GRPO. Across 200 paired cases, PI-GRPO improves IAE in 75 and worsens it in 125. Phase margin rises for C1 from 76.21° to 80.48° (p.30).
- Setup B, training: validation Pass@1 peaks at 54.5% at step 100 and falls to 19.0% by step 800 (over-optimization, pp.27–28). In other words, online reward is not a reliable checkpoint criterion.
- Setup B against IMC: there is no batch comparison. In Table S2, IMC still has the lowest IAE in C1, C2 and C4. In C3, SFT (185.05) and GRPO (196.11) are both above IMC (176.05).

## 7. Terms to define
- FOPDT / SOPDT: first- or second-order lag plus pure dead-time transfer-function process models.
- IMC tuning: internal-model-control PID rule; the filter λ = m·max(θ, 0.1Tc), with m = 0.8, 2 or 5 for aggressive, balanced or conservative (p.41).
- Pass@k: % of test cases in which at least one of the first k recommended gain sets meets all acceptance criteria. Higher is better.
- Acceptance criteria: finite response, settled within ±5%, overshoot <15%, steady-state error <1% (p.8).
- Relative delay ρ: θ/T (FOPDT) or θ/(τ1+τ2) (SOPDT).
- Dynamic prompt: prompt rebuilt each round from measured response features, diagnoses and feedback, with demonstrations matched to the requested style.
- SFT: supervised fine-tuning on IMC-target examples.
- GRPO / PI-GRPO: group relative policy optimization, an RL method comparing 4 candidates per prompt. PI-GRPO is the physics-informed version with a stability-gated reward.
- Routh–Hurwitz with Padé: algebraic stability test on the closed-loop polynomial, with the delay replaced by a [1/1] Padé approximation.
- KPI-3 / Numeric-8: ablated prompts with 3 headline indicators, or all 8 numbers without diagnostic text.
- QLoRA: 4-bit quantized low-rank adapter fine-tuning.
