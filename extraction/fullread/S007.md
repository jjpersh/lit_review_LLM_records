## 2. Eligibility
Yes, in an adjacent domain. A team of pre-trained LLM agents tunes the gains of conventional controllers (P/PID and full-state feedback). It does this by proposing parameters, which are scored by closed-loop simulation. The plants are textbook mechatronic benchmarks: DC motor position, ball and beam, inverted pendulum, and double inverted pendulum. None of them is a process system. The LLM works at design time and offline, not in the running loop. It sets the controller gains that a conventional controller then uses. Recommend `core`, flagged as adjacent (mechatronics/motor), with four tested setups (one per plant).

## 3. What was built, in plain words
All plants are simulated from ODE models (Appendix B, p.13). No hardware is used. In every case a conventional feedback law moves the actuator continuously: a P/PI/PD/PID controller, or full-state feedback (FSF, u = −K·x). The LLMs never touch the actuator. They only choose the controller's gains.

AgenticControl is a LangGraph workflow of six LLM "agents" that exchange JSON (p.3–4):
- **LLMSelector** picks the controller type and the gain ranges.
- **LLMScenarist** picks the test scenario: nominal, noise, disturbance, or parameter uncertainty.
- **LLMActor** proposes a gain set. Its inputs are the system description, the allowed ranges, a buffer of past attempts and metrics, and feedback from the Critic and Juror (Fig. 5, p.6).
- The proposed gains are simulated, and the code computes MSE, settling time, overshoot, rise time, zero crossings, control effort and a stability flag (p.5).
- **LLMCritic** reads the metrics and tells the Actor to EXPLORE or EXPLOIT.
- **LLMTerminator** decides CONTINUE, TERMINATE_SUCCESS or TERMINATE_REDESIGN.
- **LLMJuror** handles failed or stalled loops. It is given statistics on the Actor's parameter choices and can widen or shift the gain ranges (RECONSIDER_RANGE) or approve a switch of controller type.

This loop is an "actor-critic" pattern in name only: there is no learning and no gradient. It is in-context, iterative trial-and-error with a simulator in the loop. Scenarios become harder only after the current one succeeds (p.5).

The only check on LLM outputs is the simulation score itself, plus the gain ranges written into the prompt. The paper does not describe a code-level clamp or validator.

For the reported experiments, the authors say the Selector and Scenarist "are replaced by manual specifications", and target metrics and maximum iterations are set by hand (p.4). The appendix logs still show Selector and Scenarist prompts and responses (pp.14–16, 32–37), so it is unclear which parts were LLM-driven.

No fine-tuning is used. The full prompts are given in the appendix. The model behind cases 1–3 is not named. Five models are compared on the double inverted pendulum (p.9).

## 4. Each tested setup
1. **DC motor position, P controller (p.6–7, logs pp.14–32).** Nominal scenario only. One optimization run of 27 iterations. Targets: MSE 0.9, Ts 3.0 s, overshoot 10%. No comparator. Metrics: MSE, Ts, Mp.
2. **Ball and beam, FSF (p.7–8, logs pp.32–48).** Three scenarios: nominal; noise with disturbance; 20% parameter uncertainty (p.37). One design run per scenario (14, 5 and 47 iterations, with two range reconsiderations). The final gains were then evaluated by 100 Monte Carlo runs. Comparator: LQR with Q = diag(100,100,10,10), R = 1. The weights were chosen by the authors, and the paper does not say how they were tuned. Metrics: MSE, Ts, Mp (Table 2).
3. **Inverted pendulum, PID then FSF (p.7–9).** Three scenarios: nominal, measurement noise, uncertainty with disturbance. Iterations were deliberately capped at 4 per cycle to exercise the Juror. PID failed to reach its targets after 44 iterations and 10 range changes, and the framework switched to FSF. Evaluation used Monte Carlo with 100 runs. Comparators:
   - LQR (Q = diag(10,0), R = 0.1).
   - MATLAB/Simulink PIDTuner (Kp = 1.18, Ki = 1.94, Kd = 0.18). How PIDTuner was configured is not reported, so it may be a default tune.

   Metrics: MSE, Ts, Mp (Table 3).
4. **Double inverted pendulum, FSF, five LLMs (p.9–10).** Models: DeepSeek-V3, DeepSeek-R1-70B, LLaMA-4-17B, GPT-4o mini and Gemma2-27B, with 10 independent runs per model. Fig. 9 shows means and standard deviations. Table 4 reports each model's best run (minimum weighted sum of MSE, Mp and Ts), compared against LQR (Q = diag(1,1,10,10), R = 10). No off-nominal scenario is described. Metrics: MSE, Mp, Ts, control effort, and iterations to convergence.

## 5. Results
- **DC motor.** "Kp = 12.75 as the best parameter, achieving an MSE of 0.9103, settling time of 3.76 s, and overshoot of 10.98%" (p.6). None of the three targets was met. The Terminator nonetheless issued TERMINATE_SUCCESS, noting "despite not fully meeting all target metrics" (p.7). The log confirms this: "While the target metrics are not fully met…" (p.32). There is no baseline.
- **Ball and beam vs LQR** (Table 2, p.8; MC, 100 runs; lower is better for all three metrics):

  | Scenario | AgenticControl MSE / Mp | LQR MSE / Mp |
  |---|---|---|
  | Nominal | 0.0498 / 11.87% | 0.0552 / 0.00% |
  | Noise + disturbance | 0.0500 / 13.89% | 0.0552 / 3.45% |
  | Uncertain parameters | 0.0508 / 12.78% | 0.0558 / 0.03% |

  LQR settling times were also shorter (3.61 / 4.06 / 3.53 s vs 3.83 / 4.24 / 3.89 s). The text says the design "outperforms… LQR… in MSE, though with higher overshoot" (p.7). The 5% overshoot target was missed in every scenario of Table 2.
- **Inverted pendulum, Juror effect.** "reducing PID's MSE in Scenario 1 from 0.4817 to 0.2694 over 44 iterations with 10 range adjustments" (p.8). The PID targets were still not met.
- **Inverted pendulum, nominal** (Table 3, p.8; MC, 100 runs):

  | Controller | MSE | Ts | Mp |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | FSF (AgenticControl) | 0.2531 | 0.60 s | 4.30% |
  | LQR | 0.2529 | 0.59 s | 1.44% |
  | PID (AgenticControl) | 0.2694 | 2.27 s | 23.63% |
  | PIDTuner | 0.4566 | 2.73 s | 64.27% |

- **Inverted pendulum, other scenarios.**
  - Noise: FSF MSE 0.3268 vs LQR 0.3268 vs PIDTuner 0.5897.
  - Uncertainty with disturbance: FSF 0.4254 vs LQR 0.4054 vs PIDTuner 1.5707. LQR is better on MSE here, and FSF is better on Mp (2.65% vs 4.52%).
- **The abstract's "55%" claim.** The abstract claims PID "reducing PID tracking error by 55%" against PIDTuner (p.1). This cannot be reproduced from Table 3. The nominal-scenario MSE ratios are:
  - PID vs PIDTuner (0.2694/0.4566): about a 41% reduction.
  - FSF vs PIDTuner (0.2531/0.4566): about a 45% reduction.
- **Double inverted pendulum, convergence.** "DeepSeek models converge around 50 iterations, LLaMA-4-17B and GPT-4o mini near 120, while Gemma2-27B fails to meet performance targets" (p.9). "All models struggle to achieve the 20% overshoot target" (p.9).
- **Double inverted pendulum, best runs** (Table 4, p.10; best of 10 runs per model; lower is better):

  | Method | MSE | Mp | Ts |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | DeepSeek-V3 | 0.1328 | 19.16% | 1.14 s |
  | GPT-4o mini | 0.1395 | 16.53% | 1.10 s |
  | DeepSeek-R1-70B | 0.1645 | 13.32% | 1.12 s |
  | LLaMA-4-17B | 0.1823 | 18.82% | 0.78 s |
  | Gemma2-27B | 0.1972 | 18.66% | 1.80 s |
  | LQR | 0.1500 | 31.83% | 1.71 s |

  The text says "all LLM models demonstrate superior settling time and control effort compared to the LQR solution" (p.9). Gemma's Ts (1.80 s) is in fact worse than LQR's (1.71 s). The control-effort differences are about 1 in 1573. Comparing the best of 10 runs against one LQR design favours the LLMs.
- **LLM vs non-LLM.** The paper shows the LLM-tuned controllers beating a non-LLM alternative in these cases:
  - MATLAB PIDTuner, on MSE, Ts and Mp in all three inverted-pendulum scenarios. The PIDTuner setup may be untuned or default.
  - LQR, on MSE only, for the ball and beam (worse Mp and Ts).
  - LQR, on Mp and Ts for the double inverted pendulum (best of 10 runs).

  Against LQR the inverted-pendulum FSF is roughly a tie. The LQR weights are arbitrary choices, so beating LQR on MSE or Mp is a statement about the chosen weights, not about optimality.
- **Not reported.** LLM latency, token counts and cost are not given. There are no counts of invalid or out-of-range proposals.

## 7. Terms to define
- **FSF (full-state feedback):** u = −K·x with hand-searched gains K1…K4. For the inverted pendulum it acts like PD control with a filtered velocity estimate (p.9).
- **LQR:** linear quadratic regulator, the optimal state-feedback gain for the weights Q and R. Used as the baseline.
- **PIDTuner:** MATLAB/Simulink's automatic PID tuning app. Used as the PID baseline.
- **Actor-critic loop (here):** an LLM proposes gains, and an LLM critiques the simulated metrics. It is not RL training.
- **EXPLORE/EXPLOIT:** the Critic's instruction to search widely or to refine locally.
- **RECONSIDER_RANGE:** the Juror's decision to move the allowed gain intervals.
- **TERMINATE_SUCCESS / TERMINATE_REDESIGN:** the Terminator's decisions to stop with the current controller, or to switch controller type.
- **Scenario I/II/III:** nominal; noise (with disturbance); parameter uncertainty (with disturbance).
- **Zero crossings:** the number of sign changes of the error signal. Used as an oscillation indicator.
- **Monte Carlo (100 runs):** repeated simulation of the final gains under random noise and parameters. It is not a repeat of the LLM design process.
