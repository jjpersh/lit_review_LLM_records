## 2. Eligibility
Eligible, core. A fine-tuned generative LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with LoRA adapters) works as an online parameter estimator inside an adaptive NMPC loop. At scheduled instants it outputs the uncertain model parameters, and the NMPC predictor then uses them (pp.2, 5, 7). Two closed-loop process simulations are tested: a non-isothermal CSTR and a WWTP benchmark. These are process systems, not an adjacent domain. The LLM is used as a numeric sequence-to-parameter regressor, not as a reasoning or language agent.

## 3. What was built, in plain words
The plants are simulations only. Case 1 is a SISO non-isothermal CSTR: reactor temperature is controlled by coolant flow F_j, and the uncertain parameters are k0, UA and c_p,j. Case 2 is a MIMO wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) with 6 states and 3 inputs (q2, qp, fk). It controls heterotrophic biomass x_w, substrate s_w and dissolved oxygen c_w, and its uncertain parameters are μm, kLa and n_nr (pp.10, 13, 15).

A conventional NMPC moves the actuators continuously. It is solved with CasADi/IPOPT, uses an LQR-derived terminal cost and a soft terminal Lyapunov region, and recomputes the terminal ingredients each time a parameter update is accepted (pp.3–5). The LLM replaces the moving-horizon estimator (MHE).

Once a full observation window is available, each state and input channel is compressed into fifth-degree polynomial coefficients. These are serialized into a chat-style prompt. The fine-tuned model returns a fixed key-value string "theta_1=<float>, …" (pp.2, 6).

Each output goes through four steps before the NMPC uses it:
1. It is parsed.
2. A smooth "soft-clip" saturation keeps each parameter within nominal ± z_max·σ.
3. The model is re-simulated over the window, and a regularized normalized window error is computed.
4. The update is rejected if it does not improve the current model fit (pp.3, 7).

Optional variants insert a local correction before the gate: stochastic random search (SR) or Powell minimization seeded with the LLM estimate, or a second "refiner" LLM (Dual) (pp.7–8). The LLM is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank r, q_proj/v_proj) on Monte Carlo simulated windows on an A100 GPU. There is no human in the loop.

## 4. Each tested setup
**Setup A: CSTR (simulated), LLM-based estimator feeding the NMPC model.**
- Runs: 20 Monte Carlo replicates per method, all using the same fixed scenario table. The scenarios include parametric mismatch, regime changes and Gaussian measurement noise, and the reference makes four setpoint steps (pp.8–9).
- Comparators: a null benchmark with fixed nominal parameters; a CasADi/IPOPT nonlinear least-squares estimator with multistart and warm start; Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO); an MLP trained on the same polynomial features; and standalone Powell and SR ablations without an LLM seed (pp.2, 9). IPOPT is a properly configured non-LLM optimizer.
- What was varied: estimator, with 8 LLM variants: LLM, LLM+SR, LLM+Powell, Dual, Dual+SR, Dual+Powell, Qwen-Math, and Qwen-Math Dual.
- Metrics: IAE, ISE, MAE and Std. Error, each normalized to the null benchmark; MAPE of the parameters; and wall-clock time. Statistics: Friedman, Mann–Whitney with Cliff's δ, Welch–Holm, and TOST at a ±5% margin (p.8).

**Setup B: WWTP (simulated, MIMO), same architecture and variants.**
- Runs: 20 Monte Carlo runs with stochastic influent disturbances and parametric mismatch (p.13).
- Comparators: the same as Setup A.
- NMPC horizons: Np = 40 and Nc = 15 (p.14). Parameters are updated every 60 steps (J = 60; p.16).

## 5. Results
**CSTR control performance**
- All effective estimators are tightly bunched (p.9): "normalized IAE and MAE concentrated in the narrow range 0.396–0.411". Lower is better, relative to the null benchmark = 1. Each value is a mean over 20 runs.
- The single-stage LLM has the best ISE (p.9): "The smallest ISE and Std. Error are obtained by the single-stage LLM estimator (0.278 ± 0.014…)". IPOPT's ISE is 0.283 ± 0.020 (Table 2, p.11).
- Equivalence to IPOPT (p.11): "10 of the 12 non-IPOPT methods are equivalent to IPOPT for IAE, MAE, and Std. Error under a ±5% Null-normalized margin". For ISE, 9 of 12 are equivalent.
- The paper concludes parity, not superiority (p.12): "LLM estimators do not outperform the classical solvers, but that they reach the same closed-loop performance regime".
- Control-only ranking (Table 6, p.13; lower is better): "LLM ranks first with a control-only score of 0.0057, closely followed by IPOPT (0.0060)" (p.12). The score is a weighted sum of ISE/IAE/StdErr/MAE.
- The LLM seed helps the local refiners. Standalone Powell gets IAE 0.853 and SR 0.804, while LLM+Powell gets 0.396 (Table 2, p.11).
- Latency (p.11): "LLM single-stage variants require 2.183 s per call (Qwen2.5–0.5B-Instruct) or 2.602 s (Qwen-Math)". The comparison values are IPOPT 0.139 s and MLP 0.008 s; Dual variants take 4.5–5.6 s.
- Parameter MAPE: k0 errors fall in a range of "6.0–7.4%" across methods (p.11). By the MAPE-only ranking, GWO comes first and the LLM 10th (Table 6).

**WWTP control performance**
- The Powell-based family ties with IPOPT (p.13): "IPOPT, Powell, LLM+Powell, and LLM Dual+Powell—collapses to the same closed-loop solution, with normalized IAE… approximately 0.807".
- The LLM-only variant is worse: IAE 0.882 ± 0.055 (Table 7, p.14).
- The best non-Powell LLM variants (p.13): "LLM Dual+SR (0.834 ± 0.036 IAE) and LLM+SR (0.845 ± 0.041 IAE)".
- The math model fails (p.13): "Qwen-Math (0.984 ± 0.052 IAE; 1.079 ± 0.122 ISE) remain close to the Null benchmark".
- No equivalence (p.13): "no non-IPOPT method is formally equivalent to IPOPT". Under Welch–Holm, GWO and Qwen-Math are significantly worse than IPOPT on IAE, ISE and MAE (p.14).
- The MLP wins on parameter accuracy (p.14): "The MLP surrogate achieves the lowest errors for μm and nnr (0.936 ± 0.195% and 0.831 ± 0.166%)". The LLM-only MAPE for μm is 2.330%.
- Per-call time (p.14): "LLM-based variants require 3.89–6.94 s per call". For comparison, MLP takes 0.01 s and IPOPT 0.17 s.
- The LLM seed adds nothing to Powell (p.16): "the LLM warm-start therefore adds little to the final Powell-refined solution".

**Does an LLM beat a non-LLM alternative?**
- Not against IPOPT or MLP. In the CSTR the LLM is statistically equivalent to IPOPT; it ranks first on the composite control score, but the margin is not significant.
- In the WWTP, IPOPT/Powell and MLP are better on control and on parameter accuracy.
- The LLM variants beat GWO in the WWTP (GWO IAE 0.968). They also beat seedless Powell and SR in both cases.

## 7. Terms to define
- NMPC: nonlinear model predictive control; solves a finite-horizon nonlinear optimal-control problem at each sample.
- MHE: moving horizon estimation; estimates states and parameters by optimization over a past data window.
- Sequence-to-Parameter estimator: a model that maps a window of input–output data directly to parameter values.
- LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation; fine-tunes a small set of added low-rank weights in an LLM.
- Soft-clip: a smooth saturation that keeps each estimate within nominal ± z_max·σ.
- Window-error gate: re-simulates the window with the candidate parameters and accepts them only if the fit improves.
- SR (stochastic refinement): random multiplicative perturbations of the seed, keeping the best-scoring candidate.
- Dual LLM: a second fine-tuned LLM that corrects the first LLM's estimate using residual features.
- GWO: Grey Wolf Optimizer, a population-based metaheuristic.
- TOST: two one-sided tests for equivalence within a margin (here ±5% of the null-normalized metric).
- Cliff's δ: a nonparametric effect size; |δ| < 0.147 counts as negligible.
- MAPE: mean absolute percentage error of the parameter estimates against the true values.
- Null benchmark: NMPC with fixed nominal parameters and no estimation.
